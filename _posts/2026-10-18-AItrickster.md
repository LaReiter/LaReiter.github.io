When I first started programming, I learned about two kinds of errors:

* Syntax errors
* Semantic errors

Syntactically, today's AI models rarely get it wrong.

But semantically, we who are using AI as our personal engineers and coding assistants have to be more careful than ever.

Back in the days, you did both the thinking and the coding. That gave you time to reason on every small incremental change and its effect.

Today, we are just messengers. We are giving the instructions and hope that they were clear enough for the model to succeed with the intended task.

Unforunately, we can’t validate if an AI understood what was inside our brain. And it doesn’t get context the way a human being does.  

Therefore, to eliminate the increased risk of semantic errors, we need to test the AI rigorously:

We need to make small sanity checks all the time.
But also larger validation tests. How does this AI solution work on something which I know the real solution to?
It is important that we do not let AI decide on the test and the criteria for fulfilling these.
If there are errors in semantics, then it might not understand fully what to test and how to pass.

We need to become devilish good at designing traps and tests to check if your model has created the product we want it to.
